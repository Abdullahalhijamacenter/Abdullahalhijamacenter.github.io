What is Hijama?

Hijama is the Arabic name for cupping, a traditional therapy used for centuries in the Middle East, South Asia and China. A cup is placed on the skin and suction draws the skin upward. In wet cupping, tiny surface cuts let a small amount of blood collect in the cup. In dry cupping there are no cuts.

Many Muslims value Hijama because it is a practice recorded in the Prophetic tradition.

An honest note

Research suggests cupping may ease some types of pain, but the evidence is limited and mostly low quality. Hijama is a complementary therapy and does not replace a doctor's care.

Understanding cupping

Tradition

Cupping is used in traditional medicine across the Middle East and China. Suction is made with glass, plastic or silicone cups and a pump.

What research says

Reviews suggest possible short-term pain relief, for example in neck and back pain, but studies are small and of low quality. It is not a cure-all.

Safety matters

Risks include bruising, skin marks, burns and infection. Equipment shared without sterilisation can spread blood-borne disease, so clean, single-use supplies are essential.

Please speak to your doctor first if you are pregnant, take blood thinners, or have a bleeding disorder, anaemia, heart, liver or kidney disease, or a skin condition such as eczema or psoriasis.

What to expect

  1. Talk first. Share your health history, medicines and what you hope to get from the visit.
  2. Preparation. The skin is cleaned and suitable areas of soft muscle, such as the back, are chosen.
  3. Cupping. Cups are placed and removed by your practitioner, who checks how you feel throughout.
  4. Aftercare. Round marks are normal and fade in days. Follow the advice you are given, and tell a doctor if you feel unwell.

Before you book
